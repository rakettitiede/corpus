# Glossary

Words we use precisely, and a few we regularly misuse.

**Suggestion** — a word somebody sent us. A suggestion exists as a thing that happened even
if we never stored it: an over-long one and a repeat of a word we already know are both
suggestions, and both vanish.

**Entry** — a suggestion an admin has approved. Only entries can be published. Approving is
the moment a suggestion becomes an entry, and it is the moment a named person becomes
answerable for it.

**Published** — visible to the partner right now. An entry is published only if it is among
the first ten by approval time. An approved word that is not published is *waiting*, and
nobody is told that either.

**The curated list** — the ten published entries. When the partner says "the list", or when
we say "the list is full", this is what is meant.

**The widget** — the partner's sidebar box that displays the curated list. Ten slots, narrow.
Their code, our data.

**Declined** — an admin looked at a suggestion and said no. In principle a decision about
one suggestion; in practice permanent for that word, because a declined word blocks every
later suggestion of the same word. Nobody planned that as a ban, but it works as one.

**Pending vs declined.** We talk about three outcomes — pending, approved, declined — but
only two of them are a decision. What is stored is whether a word was approved, and when
somebody decided. A word nobody has looked at yet and a word that was turned down are both
"not approved"; the difference is that one of them has a date and a name against it. So
"has this been declined?" is really "has anyone decided, and did they say no?"

**Banned** — we do not have this. Admins sometimes say "I banned it", meaning declined.
There is no ban, no blocklist and no notion of a blocked person: we do not know who
suggests anything.

**Approver** — the person named on a decision. The agreement means a human being here, not a
role and not a system account. "Approved by admin" would not satisfy the clause.

**The same word** — two suggestions are the same word if they match after lower-casing and
collapsing runs of whitespace. So *Petrichor*, *petrichor* and *PETRICHOR* are one word. This
matters for deciding what is a duplicate; it is not how the word is displayed. We publish
exactly the letters the suggester typed, capitals included, because a good number of the
words worth publishing are proper nouns or derived from them, and they read as mistakes
once they are flattened.
