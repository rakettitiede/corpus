# What Lexicon is

Anyone on the internet can suggest an English word. Two of our people decide whether it is
any good. The words that survive become a short public list of interesting English words.

## Who the list is actually for

The list has one consumer that matters: **the partner**, a vocabulary and reading site used
by primary and secondary schools. They embed our list as a narrow sidebar box next to their
lesson pages, headed *"Words of the Day"*. Their pupils are roughly 9–15 years old.

The partner pays an annual fee for the list. That fee is the reason this service exists. The
public suggestion form is only the cheapest way we found to fill it: the words are free, the
moderation is what costs us.

## What we promised them

The partner agreement was signed in March 2025 and runs to March 2027. Two clauses shape
almost every rule in this service:

**Brand safety.** Every word we publish must have been approved by a named person at
Lexicon. The clause says *person*, deliberately: their lawyers wanted someone answerable if
something unsuitable appears next to schoolwork. This is why nothing here is automatic, and
why "publish first, remove later" is not an option — see
`decisions/0001-no-auto-approval.md`.

**Presentation.** The box is narrow and it is fixed at ten words. Their front end caches
our list, so a list that reshuffles between page loads shows up as flicker. They asked for
a list that grows slowly and does not reorder.

## What this service is not

It is not a dictionary, and we make no claim that a published word is correctly spelled or
usefully defined — there are no definitions at all, just words. It is not a community
platform: there are no accounts, no profiles, no way to see your own suggestions and no
notifications. People suggest words into what is, from their side, a void. That is
intentional and it has a cost we accepted knowingly — see
`decisions/0002-silent-drop-for-declined-text.md`.
